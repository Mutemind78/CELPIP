🗣️ Speaking Section

Build clear, organized spoken responses for all 8 CELPIP Speaking tasks. Learn how to generate ideas quickly, develop them with reasons and examples, adapt your tone, and speak clearly under time pressure.

Start here

Speaking time: 15 minutes. The eight Speaking tasks are completed in a single 15-minute component, so you need to be ready to respond accurately from the very first task.

The eight CELPIP Speaking tasks are genuinely different. Giving advice to a friend calls for a different tone and structure than describing an unusual image, and what works for one task does not always work for another. That is why the most effective approach is to understand what each task expects, then adapt your response structure to match it.

Each task page below explains what the task requires, how to structure an answer, useful language, common traps to avoid, and finishes with an original practice prompt and an annotated model response that you can study.

The 4 Speaking Performance Areas

CELPIP Speaking is evaluated on four performance areas. These names are official CELPIP criteria. The bullet points under each one are SelfPIP study guidance about what to focus on when preparing — not a scoring formula.

Content / Coherence

Ask yourself: Can the listener easily follow and understand my ideas?

  • Number of ideas — give enough to cover the topic
  • Quality of ideas — choose ideas that are relevant and specific
  • Organization — arrange ideas so the listener can follow
  • Supporting details — explain why and how
  • Examples — concrete examples make ideas memorable

Vocabulary

Goal: precise + natural + appropriate — not "big" words.

  • Word choice — pick words that mean exactly what you intend
  • Range — vary your vocabulary across the response
  • Precision — say exactly what happened, not vaguely
  • Accuracy — use words in their correct sense
  • Natural use — let words flow as you would speak them

Do not force advanced words you are not comfortable with — a clear, accurate word is stronger than an uncertain "fancy" one.

Listenability

The goal is understandable, natural speech.

  • Rhythm — stress important words, not every word equally
  • Pronunciation — make sounds clear and distinct
  • Intonation — let your pitch rise and fall naturally
  • Pauses — short natural pauses between ideas
  • Self-correction — fix small slips and keep going
  • Grammar and sentence structure — variety, not one pattern

Listenability is not the same as having a particular accent. CELPIP does not ask you to imitate a Canadian accent — it asks whether the listener can understand you.

Task Fulfillment

Ask yourself: Did I actually do what the task asked me to do?

  • Relevance — stay on the task, not on a related story
  • Completeness — cover what the prompt asks for
  • Tone — match the situation (sympathetic, persuasive, descriptive...)
  • Length — use the time well and give enough detail

Speaking Formula System

These short frameworks are SelfPIP memory aids, not official CELPIP requirements. Pick the framework for your task, fill in your own ideas, and develop one point fully before moving on.

Task 1 CARE
  • Context — what's happening
  • Advice — your clear recommendation
  • Reason — why it helps
  • Example — when it worked (or would work)
Task 2 TIMELINE
  • Trigger — what started it
  • Build-up — what happened next
  • Climax — the main moment
  • Outcome — result and feeling
Task 3 WHAT + WHERE
  • Who / What — people and objects
  • Where — location and layout
  • Action — what's happening now
  • Perspective — how to guide a listener around
Task 4 REASONS
  • Observe — what the scene shows
  • Predict — what may happen next
  • Reasons — why you think so
  • Evidence — clues from the image
Task 5 CHOOSE
  • Choice — which side you prefer
  • Options — compare the two paths
  • How — how to achieve your choice
  • Bonus — benefits of your choice
Task 6 TACT
  • Tone — calm and respectful
  • Acknowledge — the other person's point
  • Clarify — the real issue
  • Together — propose a fair solution
  • Thank — close politely
Task 7 OPINION+
  • Opinion — state your position
  • Examples — specific instances
  • Reasons — why you believe it
  • Wider view — how common / important it is
Task 8 CLEAR
  • Context — setting and time
  • Entities — who / what is present
  • Action — what they're doing
  • Result — the unusual part / outcome

Pronunciation & Listenability

You do not need to hide your accent. You need to be understood. Focus on the sounds and rhythm that matter most for clarity.

Target sounds

Practice the contrasts that most affect clarity.

  • TH sounds: think vs this vs they're
  • R and L: keep them distinct, do not merge them
  • V and W: very vs winter
  • ED and S endings: make the final consonant clear

Word & Sentence Rhythm

Natural rhythm makes speech easier to follow.

  • Stress content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives)
  • Shorten or skip function words (the, and, very)
  • Use rising/falling intonation to show meaning
  • Plan small pauses between ideas — not mid-word

Tip: record yourself describing a task for 60 seconds. If you can follow it, the listener probably can too.

Fluency Without Memorizing

Fluency is smooth, controlled delivery — not a memorized script. Memorized answers sound robotic and earn lower marks for spontaneity.

  • Think in English. Do not translate word-for-word from your first language.
  • Keep a short mental list of connector phrases: "in my experience," "this is because," "for example."
  • Planned pauses are better than fillers. A two-second pause is fine.
  • If you forget a word: say "let me think," then continue with a simpler word.
  • It is okay to self-correct once. Repeated erasing sounds choppy.

What to Do When You Make a Mistake

Everyone slips on a word. The difference between a low score and a strong one is how you recover. The rule below is universal: keep going.

⏸️

Do not freeze. Pause for one beat, then continue. Silence is scored as lost time.

🔄

Self-correct once. Say "I mean" or "I mean to say," then move on. Do not erase repeatedly.

🔍

Re-aim at the task. If you went off track, circle back: "Getting back to my point…" or "To answer your question…"

🗣️

Use easier words. If you lose the advanced word, substitute a simpler one. A clear simple word scores higher than a broken complex one.

Remember: the examiner will not help you, and the recording keeps running. Your only tool is forward momentum.

Speaking Golden Rules

Eight rules to keep you consistent across every task.

1 Answer the question directly. Do not restate the prompt.
2 Keep one idea running for 20–30 seconds before moving on.
3 Use the task-appropriate framework from the Formula System above.
4 Stress content words. Let function words go light and quick.
5 If stuck, buy time honestly: "That's an interesting question." Then continue.
6 Match the tone: sympathetic (Advice), descriptive (Scene), persuasive (Compare), opinionated (Opinion).
7 Do not memorize. Adapt your memorized framework to your own ideas.
8 End cleanly. A short closing sentence shows control.

Practice System

Three levels build on each other. Spend real time at each before moving on — skipping ahead is the most common cause of wasted practice.

Level 1 — Say the framework aloud

Pick one task. Read the cue, run through the formula steps out loud, and time yourself to 60–90 seconds. Don't chase perfection; the goal is structure and timing.

Do one task per day until you can hit the time without hesitating on the framework itself.

Level 2 — Add your real ideas

Use the same framework, but replace placeholder thinking with your own opinions, experiences, and word choices. Record yourself.

Listen back and check it against the self-review list below.

Level 3 — Pressure test

Simulate exam conditions: a 15-minute timer, no notes, one take per task. Review with the checklist, then mark the mistakes and repeat the weak tasks.

Keep a simple log: which task, which framework, one thing that improved, one thing to fix next time.

Self-Review Checklist

After each practice recording, listen back and check what you actually did against what the task needs.

0 unchecked

Write down one task that improved and one target for the next recording.
