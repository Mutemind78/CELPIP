Writing Task 1

Writing an Email

Write a clear email for a specific situation while matching the purpose, audience, tone, and required information.

Task 1 — Writing an Email SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: Answer every part of the prompt. Audience + Purpose + Situation + Required Actions.
1 What this task is

What Is This Task?

In Task 1 you write an email in response to a specific situation. The prompt usually describes a scenario and asks you to communicate with a particular person about a particular issue, request something, explain something, or follow up on something.

Before you write anything, identify four things:

Audience

Who is the email to? A manager, a coworker, a service provider, a community contact, a friend? The reader shapes your tone and level of formality.

Purpose

Why are you writing? To request, explain, complain, apologize, thank, follow up, or propose? One email usually has one main purpose.

Situation

What happened? What is the background? What facts, dates, or details are relevant?

Required actions

What must you include? The prompt usually lists the points you must address. Missing one is a common error.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: Task 1 officially requires writing an email. The structure, examples, and practice prompts on this page are SelfPIP study guidance, not official CELPIP rules.

3 SelfPIP guidance

Email Structure

Use a flexible structure rather than one memorized template. The exact greeting and sign-off depend on the relationship.

1. Greeting

Choose an appropriate greeting based on the relationship.

  • Dear Mr. Patel,
  • Dear Ms. Chen,
  • Hello Sarah,
  • Dear Customer Service Team,

The greeting should match the situation.

2. Opening / Purpose

State why you are writing early, so the reader knows the point immediately.

Example: I am writing regarding the recent change to the community centre's schedule.

3. Body Paragraph 1

Explain the situation or problem clearly, with the details the reader needs.

4. Body Paragraph 2

Give relevant details, reasons, examples, or the requested action.

5. Closing

Clearly state what you would like to happen next or what you are hoping for.

6. Sign-off

Use an appropriate closing for the relationship.

  • Kind regards,
  • Best regards,
  • Sincerely,

Do not require one universal greeting or sign-off for every situation.

4 SelfPIP guidance

Email Tone

Tone = relationship + purpose + situation. Choose a level of formality that fits the reader and the reason you are writing.

Formal

Use when communicating with:

  • a manager
  • an organization
  • an official contact
  • an unfamiliar recipient

Neutral / Polite

Use for:

  • coworkers
  • community organizations
  • people you know professionally

Personal

Use when appropriate for:

  • friends
  • family
  • familiar contacts
Remember: the same information can be phrased very differently depending on the reader. Match the tone to the relationship — not just the topic.
5 SelfPIP guidance

Email Content Strategy

Answer every part of the prompt. The most common error is leaving out one required point.

Prompt Coverage Checklist

  • Did I address the main purpose?
  • Did I include all required points?
  • Did I explain my reasons?
  • Did I provide relevant details?
  • Did I make the requested action clear?
  • Did I use an appropriate tone?
6 SelfPIP guidance

Email Common Traps

🚨 Trap 1 — Writing a generic email

How it feels"One structure fits every situation, so I can reuse the same wording."

InsteadRead the scenario carefully. The reader, purpose, and required details should shape the email, not a template.

🚨 Trap 2 — Forgetting one required point

How it feels"The email is long enough, so I must have covered everything."

InsteadCheck the prompt point by point before submitting. One missing requirement is a common and costly error.

🚨 Trap 3 — Using the wrong tone

How it feels"The information is accurate, so the tone doesn't matter."

InsteadA complaint to a manager and a thank-you to a coworker call for different levels of formality.

🚨 Trap 4 — Writing a very long introduction

How it feels"I want to sound polite and careful, so I will explain everything up front."

InsteadState the purpose early. Save details for the body paragraphs.

🚨 Trap 5 — Repeating the same idea

How it feels"The idea is important, so I will say it a few different ways to make sure it lands."

InsteadOne clear restatement is enough. Repetition usually wastes time and lowers readability.

🚨 Trap 6 — Giving reasons without explaining them

How it feels"I already stated the reason, so it is clear enough."

InsteadA reason without a short explanation is often too thin. Connect the reason to the situation.

🚨 Trap 7 — Using complicated vocabulary incorrectly

How it feels"Advanced words will make the email sound stronger."

InsteadUse words you can use accurately. An awkward "sophisticated" word often hurts more than it helps.

🚨 Trap 8 — Ending without a clear outcome

How it feels"The email is long enough to end now."

InsteadClose by stating what you hope happens next. A vague ending leaves the reader unsure.

7 SelfPIP guidance

Email Connectors

Connectors improve logic — they should not be inserted into every sentence.

Adding
furthermore · in addition · also · moreover
Giving a reason
because · since · as · due to
Giving an example
for example · for instance · such as
Contrast
however · although · while · on the other hand
Result
therefore · as a result · consequently
Use connectors to make relationships between ideas clearer, not to sound "advanced." A sentence does not need a connector in every sentence.
8 Original SelfPIP example

Original Email Example

Original prompt (SelfPIP — not CELPIP material) A community recreation centre has changed its evening schedule, making it difficult for you to attend a class you have been attending for several months. Write an email to the centre manager. In your email: explain the situation, describe how the new schedule affects you, suggest an alternative, and ask for a response.

Planning Notes

Purpose
Explain the problem: the new evening schedule makes it difficult to attend the class.
Point 1
Explain why the new schedule is difficult — the class now starts after work finishes.
Point 2
Suggest an alternative: a slightly earlier session or a recorded option.
Point 3
Request a response.

Model Response

To:
Centre Manager, Riverbend Community Recreation Centre
From:
A regular member
Subject:
Evening schedule change — evening fitness class

Dear Centre Manager,

I am writing regarding the recent change to the community centre's evening schedule. I have been attending the evening fitness class for several months, and the new start time makes it very difficult for me to continue.

Under the previous schedule, the class began at 6:30 p.m., which allowed me to leave work and arrive in time. The new 7:45 p.m. start means I would need to leave work almost an hour later, and my current shift rarely ends before 7:00 p.m. As a result, I have missed the last two sessions.

I would be grateful if the centre could consider offering the class at 6:30 p.m. again, or perhaps at 6:00 p.m. on one additional evening each week. If neither option is possible, I would appreciate any information about a streamed or recorded version I could follow at home on the days I cannot attend in person.

Could you please let me know whether any adjustment is possible? I would be happy to discuss this further if needed.

Kind regards,
A regular member

Annotated Response

Purpose
Opening states why the writer is writing and what changed.
Situation
Explains the previous schedule and the problem it created.
Supporting detail
Gives concrete times and the result — the writer has missed the last two sessions.
Reason
Because the shift rarely ends before 7:00 p.m., the new 7:45 p.m. start does not work.
Example
Mentions missing the last two sessions as evidence.
Suggestion
Proposes an earlier session or an additional early evening, and a streamed/recorded alternative.
Request
Asks for a response and offers to discuss further.
Tone
Polite, specific, and solution-oriented — appropriate for a request to a centre manager.
SelfPIP example — not an official CELPIP response. This model shows how the planning notes became paragraphs. It is one possible approach, not a guaranteed high-scoring answer.
9 Before you submit

Task 1 Self-Check

Content

  • I answered the situation.
  • I covered every required point.
  • My ideas are relevant.

Organization

  • My paragraphs have clear purposes.
  • My ideas flow logically.

Vocabulary

  • My word choices are accurate.
  • I avoided unnecessary repetition.

Readability

  • My sentences are clear.
  • I checked grammar and punctuation.

Task Fulfillment

  • My tone matches the reader.
  • I completed the requested action.
