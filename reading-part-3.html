Reading Part 3

Reading for Information

Read an informational passage and identify main ideas, supporting details, paragraph relationships, facts, examples, and specific information.

Section 1 — What Is This Part? Official format: 9 questions SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: Read structure first, then point to the exact information the question needs.

What is this part?

You read one or more informational texts and answer questions about what the text says and where it says it.

Questions usually look for:

  • a paragraph's main idea
  • a specific fact, date, name, or number
  • the relationship between paragraphs
  • the same idea expressed in different words (paraphrase)
  • the purpose a paragraph or example serves
Official format vs SelfPIP advice: Part 3 officially contains 9 questions based on informational texts. The strategies and examples on this page are SelfPIP study guidance, not official CELPIP rules.

What I Look For

Key things to track

Main idea

The central point of a paragraph. If you had to sum it up in one sentence, what would you say?

Supporting details

Facts, numbers, examples, or descriptions that back up the main idea. A single detail is rarely the answer when the question asks for the main point.

Paragraph purpose

Why this paragraph exists: to introduce, explain, give an example, contrast, describe a sequence, or conclude.

Specific information

A single fact, date, name, amount, or statistic the question asks you to locate.

Relationships between paragraphs

Cause/effect, contrast, a new step forward, or an example following the point it supports.

Paraphrase / synonyms

The same idea in different words. The answer often rewords the passage rather than repeating it.

Tip: for "which paragraph contains...?" questions, find the idea in the question, then find the paragraph where it actually appears — even if the wording is different.
3

Strategy

Read informational texts the way the task demands, not for pleasure:

  1. 1
    Preview first.Look at paragraph purposes/structure: what is the passage about overall, and how is it organised?
  2. 2
    Note key markers.Jot the main idea of each paragraph in a few words. Do not copy sentences.
  3. 3
    Find, do not guess.Each answer must exist in the text. Point to it mentally before you choose.
  4. 4
    Match meaning to wording.The option with the most similar wording is not automatically correct. Compare meaning first.
  5. 5
    Move on quickly.If a question is taking too long, choose the best supported option and keep reading.
4 SelfPIP guidance

Common Traps

🚨 Trap 1 — Choosing a detail over the main idea

How it feels"This sentence restates key facts, so it must be the paragraph's point."

InsteadDistinguish the claim a paragraph advances (its main idea) from the facts it cites to support it.

🚨 Trap 2 — Matching one word instead of the meaning

How it feels"The option repeats a word from the passage, so it must be right."

InsteadCompare the full sentence meaning — identical words can mask a subtly different claim.

🚨 Trap 3 — Reading every sentence at the same speed

How it feels"Every line seems important, so I read the whole paragraph slowly."

InsteadSkim for structure first; slow down only on the sentence the question actually targets.

🚨 Trap 4 — Confusing an example with the main point

How it feels"The example is concrete and memorable, so it feels like the answer."

InsteadRemember: an example supports a bigger idea — it is usually evidence, not the main idea itself.

🚨 Trap 5 — Stopping at one unfamiliar word

How it feels"I don't know this word, so I have to stop and re-read everything."

InsteadUse surrounding context; if the overall meaning is clear, keep reading and return only if needed.

Remember: these traps are SelfPIP study guidance, not official rules. Use them to review your own habits after practice.

Original Mini Practice

Original SelfPIP example
Situation

A short informational article about a city community garden opening. Read the passage, then answer the questions. Answers are hidden until you choose.

Introduction

The Riverside Community Garden opened on the first Saturday of May on the corner of Maple and 5th, occupying a half-acre lot that the city had kept vacant for three years while it negotiated with neighbours over noise and parking concerns. The garden was built with a $25,000 grant from the Urban Green Fund and will be maintained by a volunteer committee of twelve residents, led by local teacher Mira Patel.

Paragraph 1

Plots are free for any resident within postcode district 4520, and each household may reserve one 4×4 foot plot for the season (May 1 to October 31). Gardeners must attend a two-hour orientation on the first or second Saturday of the month, and they must provide their own seeds and starter plants. Water access is shared through a single tap at the east end, so watering cans are provided on a first-come, first-served basis.

Paragraph 2

The volunteer committee meets every third Tuesday at 7 p.m. in the community centre to decide maintenance schedules and handle disputes. Gardeners must keep plots tidy and remove weeds weekly, and the committee may reassign a plot if a gardener is absent for more than two weeks. Compost bins are at the north corner, and the city collects garden waste every Thursday morning from April through October.

Paragraph 3

Despite strong interest, the garden will not add plots beyond the original forty-eight because utility lines under the lot restrict further digging. Instead, the committee is planning a second site at the former primary school grounds, and will ask the Urban Green Fund for an additional grant at the end of summer. Until then, interested residents are asked to join the monthly newsletter rather than wait on a list.

Q1. What is the main purpose of Paragraph 1?

Q2. Which of the following is permitted for gardeners?

Q3. According to Paragraph 3, why can the garden not add more plots?

Q4. What is one reason the correct answer to Q1 is better than option A (the expansion reason)?

Quick self-check: answer key and explanations

Q1 — B. Paragraph 1 lists reservation rules and responsibilities. Option A is a true detail, but it belongs to Paragraph 3, so it cannot be the purpose of Paragraph 1.

Q2 — B. One plot per household is stated in Paragraph 1. Options C and D add restrictions never stated; A is not supported anywhere.

Q3 — B. Paragraph 3 states utility lines restrict further digging.

Q4 — B. This is a metacognitive check: the expansion point is in Paragraph 3, so it cannot be the purpose of Paragraph 1.

How to use this page for practice: each question above has a correct answer and a tempting wrong answer built in. After you choose, read both the correct-answer explanation and the tempting-answer explanation before moving on.
