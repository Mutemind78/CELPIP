Master skimming, scanning, context clues, elimination, and deep-reading strategies to tackle all 4 parts of the CELPIP Reading module.

Start here

The four Reading parts require different approaches. You should not read every passage in exactly the same way. Learn when to skim, when to scan, when to read closely, and when to compare ideas across the text.

Each part below explains what the task tests, how to approach it, what to look for, common traps, and finishes with short original practice.

Use the same reading process every time

  1. Preview
  2. Understand the task
  3. Locate information
  4. Read the relevant section carefully
  5. Eliminate unsupported choices
  6. Answer

Reading Correspondence

Read personal or professional correspondence and understand the purpose, relationships, important details, tone, and implied meaning.

  • Skill Skim for purpose, then read closely for details and tone.
  • Watch out Matching words without checking who is writing to whom.
  • Practice An original workplace email with interactive questions.

Reading to Apply a Diagram

Reading for Viewpoints

Read a text presenting viewpoints or arguments and identify positions, reasons, supporting evidence, tone, facts, opinions, and logical relationships.

  • Skill Separate each viewpoint from the reasons behind it.
  • Watch out Treating an opinion as a fact, or the reverse.

The Core Reading Strategy

Four skills do most of the work in every Reading part. Learn what each one is for, and when to switch between them.

Skim

Read quickly, before answering anything, to understand the passage as a whole.

  • topic
  • purpose
  • structure
  • where information is located

Scan

Search with your eyes, not your voice, for the specific details a question needs.

  • names
  • dates
  • numbers
  • keywords
  • specific facts
  • locations

Read deeply

Slow down only for the lines that decide the answer.

  • two answer choices are very similar
  • the question requires inference
  • tone matters
  • the relationship between ideas matters
  • the exact meaning is important

Eliminate

Remove options you cannot support, and choose from what remains.

  • contradict the passage
  • are only partly supported
  • introduce information that was not given
  • answer a different question
  • are too broad or too narrow
Do not confuse "sounds reasonable" with "is supported by the text."

How to choose the answer

  1. A. What exactly is the question asking?
  2. B. What part of the text contains the answer?
  3. C. What does the text actually say?
  4. D. Which choice is supported?
  5. E. Eliminate choices that are unsupported, contradictory, or answer a different question.
The correct answer must be supported by the text — not simply sound logical in real life.

Time management

The official Reading component runs approximately 43–56 minutes, and the four parts are not equally long. Treat the steps below as a practised routine rather than a fixed timing rule — there is no official per-question time limit.

  1. Avoid spending too long on one question.
  2. Read the question carefully.
  3. Locate the relevant information.
  4. Compare the answer choices.
  5. Choose the answer supported by the text.
  6. Move forward.
Practice mode: when practicing, gradually train yourself to answer accurately while reducing the amount of time spent locating information.

What if I don't know a word?

Unfamiliar words are normal. Work outwards from the word instead of stopping:

  1. Read the surrounding sentence.
  2. Look for examples or explanations.
  3. Look at contrast words.
  4. Infer the approximate meaning from context.
  5. Ask whether the exact word is actually necessary to answer the question.
  6. Do not stop for every unfamiliar word.
You do not need to understand every single word to understand the passage.

Reading golden rules

1 Read with a purpose.
2 Skim before you dive deep.
3 Scan for specific information.
4 Slow down when the question requires precision.
5 Look for paraphrases, not just identical words.
6 Eliminate unsupported choices.
7 Do not let one difficult word stop you.
8 Keep moving if one question is taking too long.
