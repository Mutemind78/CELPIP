CELPIP Speaking · Task 2

Talking about a Personal Experience

Tell a clear story about a past experience using a logical sequence, relevant details, and descriptive language.

30 s preparation 60 s response SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: One event, told in order — trigger, build-up, climax, outcome — ending with what it meant to you.
1 What this task is

What Is This Task?

In Task 2 a cue asks you to talk about something that actually happened to you — a memorable trip, an important lesson, a time you took a risk, an achievement you are proud of. You narrate your own experience in your own words.

Because the story is yours, the raw material is already in your head. The real challenge is sequencing: choose ONE event, tell it from beginning to end inside the time, and finish with why it mattered or what you learned.

Preparation

30 seconds — pick one event and note the four TIMELINE beats.

Response

60 seconds — past tense, one continuous story, no list of events.

Material

your real life — no invented facts needed, so spend the time on telling.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: The task description and timings above are official CELPIP format information. The TIMELINE framework, language banks, traps, practice prompt, and model response on this page are SelfPIP study guidance — not official CELPIP material, and not a scoring rule.
2 How to answer

How to Answer This Task

The hub's TIMELINE framework gives your story four beats. Tell them in order and the story tells itself:

T — Trigger → B — Build-up → C — Climax → O — Outcome
  1. Preparation (30 s): Choose ONE event — not a general habit, not three similar events. Jot the four beats as single words: trigger, build-up, climax, outcome.
  2. Trigger (≈10 s): Open with when and why it started: "Last spring, I decided to..."
  3. Build-up (≈15 s): What happened next — keep it to two or three actions so you reach the main moment with time left.
  4. Climax (≈20 s): The key moment: what happened, who was there, what you felt. Slow down here and add one sensory detail.
  5. Outcome (≈15 s): The result AND what you took from it — the lesson or feeling is what makes the story mean something.
Timing reality: 60 seconds is roughly 100–130 spoken words. If your build-up is eating half the time, cut it — the climax and outcome must never be rushed.
3 Useful language

Useful Language

Opening the story (Trigger)
  • One experience that really stands out is...
  • I'll never forget the time I...
  • Back in [month/year], I had to...
Sequencing (Build-up)
  • At first, ... Then, ... After that, ...
  • Before I knew it, ...
  • What happened next was...
  • The plan was simple: first..., then...
The key moment (Climax)
  • That's when everything changed.
  • The moment I'll always remember is when...
  • I could hardly believe it when...
  • My heart was pounding as...
Result and reflection (Outcome)
  • In the end, ...
  • As a result, I learned that...
  • Looking back, I'm glad I...
  • It taught me the importance of...
Tense discipline

stay in the past tense throughout; only the lesson shifts to the present ("it taught me that I work well...").

Stress these words

names, places, feeling words and the outcome — time markers stay light and quick.

4 Common traps

Common Traps

⚠ Trap 1 — Picking an event that's too big

How it feels: "When I moved to Canada..." — you spend all 60 seconds on setup and never reach the main moment.

Instead: Choose a small, sharp episode (one day, one decision, one conversation) that fits in a single minute.

⚠ Trap 2 — Listing facts instead of telling

How it feels: "I went there. I ate food. I took photos. It was fun." — a timeline of facts with no story arc.

Instead: Keep the TIMELINE beats: build tension toward one climax, then resolve it. Something must CHANGE in the story.

⚠ Trap 3 — Ending before the outcome

How it feels: "...and that's when I won the race." — you stop at the climax; result and reflection are missing.

Instead: Reserve the last 15 seconds for result + lesson: "As a result, I learned that..." — that's the part that sounds mature.

⚠ Trap 4 — Drifting into present tense

How it feels: "So I'm walking down the street and I see him..." — natural in speech, but constant drift weakens control.

Instead: Anchor each beat in past tense (I went / I realized / it was). Present tense is fine only for brief vivid moments.

5 Practice

Original Practice Prompt

Original SelfPIP prompt — not an official CELPIP question

Cue: Describe a time when you had to learn something quickly in order to succeed. Say what it was, how you learned it, and why the experience mattered to you.

Task: Talk about this personal experience. (30 s preparation → 60 s response)

How to use this prompt: Record in one take without writing full sentences — only the four TIMELINE words. If you pause while speaking, the beat order wasn't internalized yet; run it again.
6 Model response

Annotated Model Response

"One experience that really stands out is the time I had to learn how to use a full bookkeeping system in three days — I was a month into my first office job when my supervisor went on sudden medical leave and I was the only one left who could touch the payroll.

At first I panicked a little and just clicked through the screens randomly. Then I realized I had to slow down, so I spent the first evening with the manual open beside me, page by page, until the logic of the system started to click.

By the third day, everything came down to one moment: the payroll deadline, five minutes to spare, and me hitting 'submit' with my heart pounding. It went through — every payment landed correctly.

Looking back, I learned that I'm actually good at learning under pressure, as long as I stop, breathe, and take it step by step. That lesson has stayed with me ever since."

Why this works

TriggerNames the event AND the stakes in the first sentence — no long wind-up, the story starts moving immediately.
Build-up"At first... Then..." carries the listener forward in past tense with two concrete actions — panic, then the manual.
Climax"everything came down to one moment" — a single vivid scene with a feeling (heart pounding) and a result. This is the story's peak.
OutcomeEnds with result + reflection ("I learned that...") — the maturity marker that lifts the whole response.

Mindset

Nobody has heard your story before — it can't be "boring" for being familiar. Tell it like you'd tell a friend at dinner: one moment, told well, beats ten moments listed.
