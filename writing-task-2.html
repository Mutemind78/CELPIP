Writing Task 2

Responding to Survey Questions

Choose and defend a position on a survey question by presenting clear reasons, relevant examples, and a logically organized argument.

Task 2 — Responding to Survey Questions SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: Choose a position that you can explain clearly. Position → Reason → Explanation → Example.
1 What this task is

What Is This Task?

In Task 2 you respond to a survey question by choosing between two options and explaining why you support your choice. The question usually presents two ways of doing something — for example, two improvements, two policies, or two approaches — and asks you to pick one and justify it.

The task is not about picking the "objectively better" option. It is about choosing a position you can defend with reasons and examples, and explaining that position clearly.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: Task 2 officially requires responding to survey questions. The structure, examples, and practice prompts on this page are SelfPIP study guidance, not official CELPIP rules.
2 Before you write

Understand the Question

Read the question carefully and identify:

What are the two choices?

Name both options in your own words so you are clear about what each one involves.

Which one do I support?

Pick the side you can explain best — not the side that just sounds "reasonable" in general.

Why do I support it?

Choose two reasons you can explain with enough detail to feel convincing.

What examples or details prove it?

For each reason, think of a concrete example, situation, or result that shows the reason in action.

Choose a position that you can explain clearly. A position you can support with two developed reasons is better than a "stronger-sounding" position you can only list vaguely.
3 SelfPIP guidance

Survey Response Structure

A flexible structure works well for most survey responses. Use it as a guide, not a rigid template.

Paragraph 1 — Position

Clearly state your choice. Tell the reader which option you support and, briefly, why.

Paragraph 2 — Reason 1

Explain your first reason. Then add a relevant example or detail that shows the reason in practice.

Paragraph 3 — Reason 2

Explain your second reason. Add a relevant example or detail as well.

Final paragraph

Briefly reinforce your position. A short closing restatement is usually enough.

Do not require this exact paragraph count for every response. Organization should serve your ideas. If one reason is very strong, it still needs its own developed paragraph.
4 SelfPIP guidance

Idea Development

A developed idea moves from the abstract to the concrete. The structure below shows one useful way to build a reason:

IDEA
→
WHY?
→
HOW?
→
EXAMPLE
→
RESULT

Example:

IDEA

Public transit is convenient.

WHY?

I can avoid driving during rush hour.

HOW?

I can use the travel time to read or prepare for work.

EXAMPLE

On busy weekdays, I can take the train instead of searching for parking.

RESULT

This saves time and reduces stress.

Simply listing reasons is weaker than developing them. For each reason, ask: why does this matter, and what does it look like in practice?
5 SelfPIP guidance

Strong Reasons vs Weak Reasons

Weak

"Option A is better because it is good."

This states a position but does not explain anything. The reader has no reason to believe it.

Better

"Option A is more convenient because it would allow commuters to avoid the heavy traffic during peak hours."

This gives a clearer reason — convenience through avoiding congestion — and it is easy to imagine a supporting example.

Why the second example works better: it identifies a specific advantage (avoiding peak traffic) and implies a concrete situation the reader can picture.
7 SelfPIP guidance

Common Survey Traps

🚨 Trap 1 — Not choosing clearly

How it feels"I can see value in both options, so I will discuss both."

InsteadPick one position and defend it. A balanced discussion that never commits is usually weaker than a clear choice with reasons.

🚨 Trap 2 — Giving both choices equal support

How it feels"I will show why each option has some strengths."

InsteadIf you support Option A, make Option A the side you are arguing for. Do not write two competing arguments and leave the reader to choose.

🚨 Trap 3 — Listing reasons without explanation

How it feels"I have three reasons, so the response is strong."

InsteadOne fully developed reason is better than three thin ones. Explain each reason and give an example where possible.

🚨 Trap 4 — Examples unrelated to the reason

How it feels"I will add any example that seems relevant to the topic."

InsteadThe example should illustrate the reason you just stated. A generic example for the topic, not the reason, weakens the paragraph.

🚨 Trap 5 — Repeating the same reason differently

How it feels"I will say the same idea two ways to make it clearer."

InsteadIf two points are really the same reason, combine them and add a second, genuinely different reason.

🚨 Trap 6 — Memorized phrases that do not fit

How it feels"Memorized phrases make the response sound prepared."

InsteadUse language that fits this specific survey. Forced phrases often sound unnatural and can hurt readability.

🚨 Trap 7 — Overly complicated vocabulary

How it feels"Complex vocabulary will impress the reader."

InsteadUse vocabulary you can use accurately. Clear, precise language is better than an awkward "sophisticated" word.

🚨 Trap 8 — Ignoring the word range

How it feels"As long as the response is complete, the length does not matter."

InsteadStay within the required range. Too short can underdevelop your ideas; too long can introduce errors.

8 Original SelfPIP example

Original Survey Example

Original prompt (SelfPIP — not CELPIP material) A city is considering two ways to improve community recreation. Option A: build more outdoor recreation spaces. Option B: expand indoor recreation facilities. Which option do you think is a better use of public funds? Explain why.

Planning Notes

Position
Option A — build more outdoor recreation spaces.
Reason 1
Accessible outdoor activities.
Explanation
Families can use them without paying membership fees.
Example
A public walking and cycling area could be used throughout the week by residents of all ages.
Reason 2
Community benefit.
Explanation
Outdoor spaces can encourage people to spend time together.

Model Response

I believe building more outdoor recreation spaces would be a better use of public funds than expanding indoor facilities. My support is based on accessibility and community benefit.

One important reason is that outdoor activities are more accessible to families. A public walking and cycling area, for example, could be used throughout the week by residents of all ages, and it would not require a membership fee. This means that people who cannot afford indoor memberships would still have a place to be active.

Another reason is the community benefit that outdoor spaces can create. When a neighbourhood has a pleasant open area, people are more likely to spend time there together, whether they are walking, jogging, or simply meeting friends. Over time, this can help a community feel more connected.

For these reasons, I support Option A. Outdoor recreation spaces can be used freely by many people, and they can bring a neighbourhood together in a way that indoor facilities are less likely to do.

Position
First paragraph states the choice clearly: Option A.
Reason 1
Accessibility — outdoor activities can be used without paying membership fees.
Example
A public walking and cycling area used throughout the week by residents of all ages.
Reason 2
Community benefit — outdoor spaces encourage people to spend time together.
Connector
"For these reasons, I support Option A."
Conclusion
Final paragraph reinforces the position and ties the two reasons together.
SelfPIP example — not an official CELPIP response. This model shows how planning notes become a developed response. It is one possible approach, not a guaranteed high-scoring answer.
9 Before you submit

Task 2 Self-Check

Task

  • Did I choose one option clearly?
  • Did I respond to the actual survey question?

Content

  • Did I support my position with reasons?
  • Did I include relevant examples or details?

Organization

  • Is my position stated early?
  • Do my paragraphs have clear purposes?

Vocabulary

  • Are my words precise and natural?
  • Did I avoid memorizing phrases that do not fit?

Readability

  • Are my sentences clear?
  • Did I check grammar and punctuation?

Task Fulfillment

  • Did I stay in the required word range?
  • Did I keep my position consistent throughout?
6 SelfPIP guidance

Survey Connectors

Connectors should be natural rather than mechanically inserted. They help the reader follow your logic.

Introducing a point
First · To begin with · One important reason is...
Adding
Furthermore · In addition · Another reason is...
Explaining
This is because... · This means that... · As a result...
Giving examples
For example... · For instance...
Concluding
Overall... · For these reasons... · Therefore...
Insert connectors where they match the logic of your ideas. A paragraph does not need a connector at the start of every sentence.
