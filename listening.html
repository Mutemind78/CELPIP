🎧 CELPIP Listening

Six task types, six different listening demands. Learn what each part asks you to do, the strategy to use, the traps to avoid, and try one short original example.

Start here

The Listening component contains six different task types (Parts 1–6). They are not the same listening job: a conversation about a problem, an everyday conversation, a factual talk, a news report, a group discussion and a set of viewpoints each ask you to listen in a different way. That is why one single strategy for every part is not the most effective approach.

Each part page explains what the task is, what to expect, what to listen for, how to answer, the traps to avoid, and finishes with a short original example you can try.

A simple approach that works for every part

  1. Understand the task
  2. Predict what matters
  3. Listen actively
  4. Track key information
  5. Eliminate traps
  6. Choose the supported answer

Part 1 8 questions

Listening to Problem Solving

Follow a conversation about a problem, understand the important details, track possible solutions, and identify the outcome.

  • Expect One conversation working through a problem and possible solutions.
  • Focus The problem, important details, suggested solutions, the final outcome.
  • Strategy Problem → Options → Discussion → Outcome.
  • Watch out Choosing the first solution mentioned.
  • Practice An original conversation with one interactive question.

Part 2 5 questions

Listening to a Daily Life Conversation

Understand everyday conversations involving plans, requests, arrangements, changes, and personal situations.

  • Expect Everyday talk about plans, requests and arrangements.
  • Focus Who is speaking and why, times, places, and what changes.
  • Strategy Understand the situation first, then track what changes.
  • Watch out Missing a change to the original plan.
  • Practice An original phone-call situation with one interactive question.

Part 3 6 questions

Listening for Information

Listen for facts, details, explanations, names, numbers, locations, times, and other important information.

  • Expect Facts and details presented in an organised way.
  • Focus Main topic, names, numbers, dates, places and reasons.
  • Strategy Listen for information, not just keywords.
  • Watch out Numbers or names attached to the wrong item.
  • Practice An original short talk with one interactive question.

Part 4 5 questions

Listening to a News Item

Follow a news report and identify the main event, important facts, background information, causes, effects, and updates.

  • Expect A short news report about a single event.
  • Focus Main event, who/what/where/when, cause and effect, updates.
  • Strategy Find the main event first, then connect the supporting facts.
  • Watch out Choosing a supporting detail as the main event.
  • Practice An original news extract with one interactive question.

Part 5 8 questions

Listening to a Discussion

Track multiple speakers, understand their ideas, recognize agreement and disagreement, and remember who said what.

  • Expect Several speakers sharing and comparing ideas.
  • Focus Who said what, positions, agreement, disagreement and reasons.
  • Strategy Track the speaker AND their position.
  • Watch out Mixing up which speaker said what.
  • Practice An original discussion with one interactive question.
Study Part 5 →
Part 6 6 questions

Listening to Viewpoints

Understand different perspectives, supporting reasons, examples, comparisons, and the difference between facts and opinions.

  • Expect One or more speakers expressing a viewpoint with support.
  • Focus Viewpoints, reasons, examples, facts vs opinions, comparisons.
  • Strategy Identify the viewpoint → find its support → compare the ideas.
  • Watch out Treating an opinion as a fact, or a fact as an opinion.
  • Practice An original commentary with one interactive question.
