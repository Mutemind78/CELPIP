CELPIP Speaking · Task 4

Making Predictions

Use the scene and available clues to predict what may happen next, explaining the reasons behind your predictions.

30 s preparation 60 s response SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: A prediction without evidence is a guess — for every "what happens next," name the clue in the image that supports it.
1 What this task is

What Is This Task?

In Task 4 the screen shows an image — the same photograph you described in Task 3 — and the prompt asks what you think will happen next. You give predictions about the people, action, or situation in the picture and explain your reasoning.

This task is evidence work. Each prediction must be anchored to something visible: body language, weather, facial expressions, what someone is reaching for. Observe first, predict second, prove it with a clue — and offer more than one prediction so the 60 seconds develop naturally.

Preparation

30 seconds — spot 2–3 clues that could drive a future event.

Response

60 seconds — future forms: will, is likely to, seems ready to.

Same image

as Task 3 — your earlier scan becomes your evidence bank.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: The task description and timings above are official CELPIP format information. The REASONS framework, language banks, traps, practice prompt, and model response on this page are SelfPIP study guidance — not official CELPIP material, and not a scoring rule.
2 How to answer

How to Answer This Task

The hub's REASONS framework keeps every prediction tied to evidence:

Observe → Predict → Reasons → Evidence
  1. Preparation (30 s): Find two or three clues in the image that point forward — someone reaching, dark clouds, a clock, a worried face. Decide two events they could cause.
  2. Observe (≈10 s): Anchor with a factual opening: "The picture shows people having a picnic in the park."
  3. Predict 1 (≈15 s): Your first prediction in future form: "I think it's likely that they'll pack up soon."
  4. Reasons + Evidence (≈20 s): Prove it — point to the specific clue: "because the sky behind them is getting dark and one of them is already holding an umbrella."
  5. Predict 2 (≈15 s): A second, connected prediction with its own clue — this is what fills the minute with development instead of one thin guess.
Timing reality: 60 seconds = about two full prediction + evidence cycles. One prediction alone will leave you stranded at the 30-second mark.
3 Useful language

Useful Language

Observation opener
  • The image shows... / In the picture, we can see...
  • From the looks of it, this is...
  • Everything about the scene suggests that...
Making predictions
  • I predict that... / I'm fairly confident that...
  • It's highly likely that... / There's a good chance that...
  • ...is bound to / is about to...
  • The most likely outcome is that...
Evidence (tying it to the image)
  • ...because / since the image shows...
  • The fact that... suggests that...
  • You can tell by his expression that...
  • The dark clouds overhead are a clear sign that...
Hedging like a native speaker
  • will (confident) → may / might (unsure)
  • probably / almost certainly / unlikely to
  • unless something unexpected happens, ...
Grammar to show off

future forms (will, be about to), conditionals ("If it keeps raining, they'll..."), and modals of probability.

Stress these words

the evidence nouns — CLOUDS, EXPRESSION, UMBRELLA — and your probability word (LIKELY, SURELY).

4 Common traps

Common Traps

⚠ Trap 1 — Describing instead of predicting

How it feels: You re-run your Task 3 description in the present tense — the clock ticks, but you never answer "what happens NEXT."

Instead: One sentence of observation, max — then move to future forms for the rest of the response.

⚠ Trap 2 — Guesses with no evidence

How it feels: "Maybe they'll go home. Maybe it will rain. Maybe someone will call." — three bare guesses, zero support.

Instead: Always pair prediction + clue: "...because the sky is turning grey behind them." Evidence is half the task.

⚠ Trap 3 — One prediction for 60 seconds

How it feels: You say the main prediction in 15 seconds, then repeat it in different words until time runs out.

Instead: Plan TWO connected predictions in prep — a near one (next few minutes) and a far one (what follows) — each with its own clue.

⚠ Trap 4 — 100% certainty (or 0%)

How it feels: "They WILL leave and it WILL storm" — absolute claims about an uncertain image sound naive; total hedging sounds empty.

Instead: Calibrate: will/bound to for the obvious, likely/probably for the reasonable, might for the speculative. Mix them.

5 Practice

Original Practice Prompt

Original SelfPIP prompt — not an official CELPIP question

Task: What do you think will happen next in this scene? Explain your predictions with evidence from the image. (30 s preparation → 60 s response)

How to use this prompt: Write down two predictions and TWO clues during prep. If any sentence you say doesn't contain a clue or a future form, it's filler — cut it.
6 Model response

Annotated Model Response

"The picture shows a family having a picnic on the grass in a park, with food laid out on a red blanket.

My first prediction is that they'll start packing up within the next few minutes — you can tell because the sky above them has turned grey, with heavy dark clouds gathering, and a few drops are already visible over the trees. A parent is likely to reach for the umbrella that's propped beside the blanket.

And I'd say there's a good chance they won't leave empty-handed: the covered dishes are still full, so someone will probably grab the food and carry it to the car before the rain really starts.

Unless the clouds blow over quickly — which can happen on days like this — I think the whole scene will be empty within twenty minutes, with nothing left on the grass except the red blanket."

Why this works

ObserveTen seconds of pure fact-setting, present tense — then the response never looks backward again.
Predict 1"they'll start packing up" + immediate evidence: grey sky, dark clouds, first drops, umbrella. Prediction and proof in one breath.
Predict 2A second, connected prediction ("won't leave empty-handed") justified by a different clue — the full dishes. Two cycles fill the minute.
EvidenceEvery claim points at something visible — never "I just feel like..." Evidence is the backbone of REASONS.
Hedge"Unless the clouds blow over" adds a calibrated alternative — sounds like a native speaker, not a gambler.

Mindset
