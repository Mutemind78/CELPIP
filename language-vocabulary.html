Active Vocabulary Beats Big Vocabulary

Mindset

Passive vocabulary is words you recognise when you read or hear them. Active vocabulary is words you can reach for while writing or speaking under pressure. CELPIP only pays for active vocabulary — a word you remember but never use in your answer helps your score zero.

The trap: knowing a fancy word helps your reading; using a correct word inside a 10-second speaking pause helps your speaking. When the two compete, always choose the word you can use instantly and accurately.

The SEE → HEAR → USE method

  • SEE — read the meaning and the example sentence on the card below. Notice the word's natural partners (its collocation).
  • HEAR — press the 🔊 button (or the underlined word) and listen once. Sound and meaning stick together.
  • USE — write your own sentence in the practice box. The box saves automatically in this browser.
  • SWAP — in your next Writing or Speaking practice, deliberately replace one basic word (good, bad, thing) with a word from these banks.
  • SEE
  • HEAR
  • USE
  • SWAP
How many words per session? Four or five words used correctly beats thirty words recognised vaguely. Learn a bank, use the words the same day, and come back tomorrow.

Ten Word Banks

See → Hear → Use every word

Bank 1 · Everyday Life

For Task 1 personal stories, emails about daily life, and speaking about routines. Saved boxes stay in this browser only.

verb / noun

Meaningto travel regularly between home and work or school

ExampleI commute to work for forty minutes every day.

Collocationa long commute · commute by train

Speaking“My commute is the worst part of my day.”

Writing“A long commute reduces the time families spend together.”

noun

Meaninga short quick job you do outside the house, such as shopping or banking

ExampleI ran a few errands before the store closed.

Collocationrun errands · a quick errand

Speaking“I have to run an errand — I will be back in ten minutes.”

Writing“Online banking saves people a trip to complete routine errands.”

noun / verb

Meaningthe amount of money you plan to spend, or the act of planning spending carefully

ExampleWe need to budget carefully this month.

Collocationstick to a budget · tighten the budget

Speaking“My budget is tight until payday.”

Writing“Families on a fixed budget feel price increases first.”

Bank 2 · Workplace

For emails to a supervisor, describing a job, and Task 3/4 workplace scenarios.

noun

Meaningthe latest time by which something must be finished

ExampleWe missed the deadline by one day.

Collocationmeet a deadline · miss a deadline · a tight deadline

Speaking“I have a deadline on Friday, so today is busy.”

Writing“Clear deadlines help teams prioritise under pressure.”

verb

Meaningto work together with other people on something

ExampleThe two departments collaborate on every launch.

Collocationcollaborate with someone on something

Speaking“We collaborate online every week.”

Writing“Schools that collaborate with local businesses prepare students better.”

Bank 3 · Opinions

For Task 2 writing and any speaking task where you must take a position.

phrase

Meaningused to introduce your personal opinion politely

ExampleIn my view, the park should stay car free.

Collocationin my view · in my opinion (slightly more formal)

Speaking“In my view, it is worth the extra cost.”

Writing“In my view, the city should invest in transit first.”

adverb

Meaningused when you believe something is probably true

ExampleIt is arguably the busiest station in the city.

Collocationarguably the best / busiest / hardest

Speaking“This is arguably the best bakery around here.”

Writing“Remote work is arguably the biggest office change since 2020.”

adjective

Meaningstrong enough to make people believe or agree

ExampleShe made a compelling case for a four day week.

Collocationa compelling reason · compelling evidence

Speaking“I find that argument quite compelling.”

Writing“The report presents compelling evidence for early action.”

Bank 4 · Problems

For complaints, emails about trouble, and any task that asks “what is wrong?”

noun

Meaninga problem that delays or slows progress

ExampleLosing the booking was a setback, but we recovered.

Collocationa minor / major setback · suffer a setback

Speaking“The delay was a setback, not a disaster.”

Writing“Each setback shows the team what the plan missed.”

noun

Meaningtrouble or annoyance that is inconvenient but not serious

ExampleSorry for the inconvenience caused by the delay.

Collocationcause inconvenience · sorry for the inconvenience

Speaking“Sorry for the inconvenience — the wait is almost over.”

Writing“Closing the lane causes real inconvenience for local residents.”

noun

Meaninga situation where there is not enough of something

ExampleThere is a shortage of nurses in the region.

Collocationa shortage of · housing / staff / water shortage

Speaking“There is a housing shortage here — prices keep climbing.”

Writing“A shortage of staff makes the service slower for everyone.”

Bank 5 · Solutions

For “what should be done?” tasks — proposals, recommendations, improvement plans.

noun

Meaninganother option you can choose instead

ExampleCycling is a cheaper alternative to driving downtown.

Collocationa viable / attractive alternative · alternative to

Speaking“Remote work is a realistic alternative for many jobs.”

Writing“The city should consider a viable alternative before raising fares.”

noun

Meaninga change that makes something better

ExampleThe new schedule made a noticeable improvement in service.

Collocationa marked / gradual improvement · room for improvement

Speaking“The buses are crowded, but service is improving.”

Writing“Small changes can lead to a noticeable improvement in public trust.”

noun

Meaninga final decision or agreement that ends a problem

ExampleBoth sides reached a resolution after a short meeting.

Collocationreach / find a resolution · peaceful resolution

Speaking“The dispute was settled by a peaceful resolution.”

Writing“A quick resolution protects both customers and staff.”

Bank 6 · Emotions

Describe people’s reactions in Part 3 questions and Part 6 opinions.

adj.

Meaningannoyed because things are not working as they should

ExampleResidents are frustrated by the slow service.

Collocationfeel / grow frustrated · frustrated with / about

Speaking“I get frustrated when meetings run over time.”

Writing“Commuters are frustrated by repeated delays.”

adj.

Meaningcalm and less worried because a problem has ended

ExampleShe was relieved when the delay ended.

Collocationvisibly / visibly relieved · relieved to hear

Speaking“I was relieved the meeting ended early.”

Writing“Parents will be relieved to see stronger safety rules.”

adj.

Meaningfeeling that there is far too much to handle

ExampleNew students often feel overwhelmed at first.

Collocationfeel / get overwhelmed · completely overwhelmed

Speaking“I felt overwhelmed by all the paperwork.”

Writing“Staff are overwhelmed by the number of requests.”

Bank 7 · Describing People & Scenes

For Part 7 describe-the-picture writing and speaking answers that need concrete detail.

adj.

Meaningable to be trusted to work well, again and again

ExampleThe night bus is reliable and cheap.

Collocationhighly reliable · a reliable source · reliable service

Speaking“I need a reliable car for work.”

Writing“A reliable internet connection is essential for remote work.”

adj.

Meaningcalm and quiet, without conflict

ExampleThe park is peaceful early in the morning.

Collocationpeaceful / quiet protest · a peaceful solution

Speaking“I like peaceful places away from traffic.”

Writing“Residents want a peaceful solution to the dispute.”

adj.

Meaningfull of busy people, movement and activity

ExampleThe market was bustling with shoppers.

Collocationbustling with activity · bustling street / market

Speaking“Downtown is always bustling on weekends.”

Writing“The bustling neighbourhood attracts young families.”

Bank 8 · Active Vocabulary (Use These)

The highest-value words in the SelfPIP set: meaning, example, collocation and ready-to-say Speaking and Writing lines. Hear each word with 🔊, then write your own sentence in the box.

noun

Meaninggeneral agreement

ExampleThere was a consensus that public transportation needed improvement.

Collocationreach a consensus · a broad consensus

Speaking“We finally reached a consensus after a long discussion.”

Writing“Governments should build consensus before changing popular policies.”

verb

Meaningachieve/reach

ExampleStudents need to work hard to attain their goals.

Collocationattain a goal · attain success

Speaking“It took me years to attain the level I wanted.”

Writing“Companies attain long-term growth by investing in their staff.”

verb

Meaningthink about carefully

ExampleI am contemplating moving closer to my workplace.

Collocationcontemplate a decision · contemplate + -ing

Speaking“I am contemplating a career change.”

Writing“City councils should contemplate the long-term effects before deciding.”

adjective

Meaningpointless; unlikely to produce results

ExampleComplaining without taking action would be futile.

Collocationa futile attempt · utterly futile

Speaking“Waiting for better weather felt futile.”

Writing“Without enforcement, new recycling rules may be futile.”

phrase

Meaningused to

ExampleI am accustomed to working long hours.

Collocationbe accustomed to + -ing · get accustomed to

Speaking“I am not accustomed to waking up so early.”

Writing“Newcomers quickly become accustomed to urban life.”

adjective

Meaningthankful

ExampleI am grateful for the opportunity.

Collocationgrateful for · grateful to someone

Speaking“I am grateful for my supportive colleagues.”

Writing“Employees feel grateful for flexible working hours.”

adjective

Meaningclever & good at judging

ExampleShe made an astute decision.

Collocationan astute observation · an astute decision

Speaking“That is an astute point.”

Writing“An astute business owner plans for economic downturns.”

adjective

Meaningold and in poor condition

ExampleThe city should renovate the dilapidated building.

Collocationa dilapidated building · dilapidated housing

Speaking“The park benches were completely dilapidated.”

Writing“Dilapidated schools harm students’ concentration.”

noun

Meaningemployees/staff

ExampleThe company needs additional personnel.

Collocationadditional personnel · personnel shortage

Speaking“The store is short on personnel at night.”

Writing“Hospitals report a shortage of qualified personnel.”

adjective

Meaningunlucky/regrettable

ExampleIt was unfortunate that the event was cancelled.

Collocationan unfortunate incident · it is unfortunate that

Speaking“That was an unfortunate misunderstanding.”

Writing“It is unfortunate that fees were raised again.”

verb

Meaningmake very interested

ExampleTechnology has always fascinated me.

Collocationbe fascinated by · deeply fascinating

Speaking“Volcanoes have always fascinated me.”

Writing“The history of the city fascinates visitors.”

adjective

Meaningconfused/puzzled

ExampleI was perplexed by the unexpected result.

Collocationbe perplexed by · remain perplexed

Speaking“I was perplexed by the instructions.”

Writing“Officials remain perplexed by the falling numbers.”

noun

Meaningstrong preference/tendency

ExampleHe has a penchant for learning new technologies.

Collocationhave a penchant for · a distinct penchant

Speaking“She has a penchant for early mornings.”

Writing“The region has a penchant for large infrastructure projects.”

Bank 9 · Understand, Don’t Force

Useful for Reading and Listening — but don’t try to force them into every Speaking answer. Understand → recognize → use naturally.

adjective

Meaningdead; formal

ExampleThe will of the deceased businessman was read in court.

noun

Meaningperson who strongly supports an idea/belief

ExampleThousands of adherents joined the movement.

adjective

Meaningextremely shocked

ExampleThe crowd was aghast at the size of the damage.

noun

Meaningclothing

ExampleGuests were asked to wear formal attire.

noun

Meaningcategory/type of music, film, literature

ExampleScience fiction is my favourite genre.

noun

Meaningunusual characteristic/habit

ExampleShe has a quirk of laughing when she is nervous.

adjective

Meaningextremely surprised

ExampleWe were astonished by the speed of the new train.

Remember: Understand → recognize → use naturally. You don’t get extra CELPIP points simply for throwing difficult words into your answer.

Bank 10 · Low Priority

Don’t waste your limited study time actively memorizing these.

noun

Meaningclose friend/associate, often with a negative implication

ExampleThe minister was accused of giving jobs to his cronies.

noun

Meaningdead human body, usually medical/scientific context

ExampleMedical students examine a cadaver in anatomy class.

noun

Meaningperson who organizes/calls a meeting

ExampleThe convener set the date for the next meeting.

verb

Meaningsaliva unintentionally coming from the mouth

ExampleThe baby drooled on the tablecloth.

adjective

Meaningextremely hungry

ExampleAfter the hike we were starving.

Know them if you encounter them, but move on. Your study minutes are better spent inside Banks 1–8.

Upgrade Your Sentences

Basic → Better

You rarely need a bigger word — you need a more exact one. Replace the vague word plus an intensifier with a single precise word, and the sentence gets shorter and stronger at the same time.

Pair 1 — vague size

BasicThe park is very big.
BetterThe park is spacious.

Pair 2 — vague quality

BasicThe bus was bad and always late.
BetterThe bus service was unreliable.

Pair 3 — vague quantity

BasicA lot of students do not finish the course.
BetterMany students fail to complete the course.
Upgrade one sentence a day. Take a sentence you wrote in practice, circle every “very / really / a lot of / thing”, and replace each one with a precise word. That is the whole method.
4

Use It in Your Practice

Cross-study

Vocabulary is only useful when it survives the clock. Build it where the clock already exists.

  • Speaking Part 7 (advice): force one bank word into every recommendation — “a viable alternative”, “a peaceful solution”, “reliable service”.
  • Writing Task 4 (reports): the Problems and Solutions banks map directly onto the “issues + recommendations” structure.
  • Speaking Part 2 (describe scene): Describing bank gives you bustling / spacious / peaceful instead of big / busy / quiet.
  • Daily habit: three words, three written sentences, one spoken sentence each — the SEE → HEAR → USE loop closes with the 🔊 button.
