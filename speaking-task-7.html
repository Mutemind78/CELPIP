CELPIP Speaking · Task 7

Expressing Opinions

State a clear opinion on a controversial issue, support it with reasons and examples, and respond to a likely objection.

30 s preparation 90 s response SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: Take a side in your first sentence — then spend the full 90 seconds developing it, not hedging around it.
1 What this task is

What Is This Task?

In Task 7 the screen shows a statement about a debatable social issue — education, work, technology, city life — and asks whether you agree or disagree. You give your opinion and support it with reasons and examples.

This is a 90-second response with only 30 seconds to prepare, which makes it the hardest timing combination in the test. You need one position, two developed reasons, and enough material to fill the time without repeating yourself. Hedging ("both sides have points") costs you — the prompt asked for YOUR opinion.

Preparation

30 seconds — choose your side immediately, then note 2 reasons.

Response

90 seconds — the longest response: opinion + 2 reasons + example.

Requirement

a clear position — agree or disagree, stated up front, held throughout.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: The task description and timings above are official CELPIP format information. The OPINION+ framework, language banks, traps, practice prompt, and model response on this page are SelfPIP study guidance — not official CELPIP material, and not a scoring rule.
2 How to answer

How to Answer This Task

The hub's OPINION+ framework fills the full 90 seconds without repetition:

Opinion → Examples → Reasons → Wider view
  1. Preparation (30 s): Choose a side in the first 5 seconds — any defensible side works. Then note TWO different reasons; two thin reasons beat one stretched one for filling time with development.
  2. Opinion (≈10 s): State the position unambiguously in sentence one: "I strongly believe that public transit should be free for students."
  3. Examples (≈25 s): Immediately ground it with a concrete instance — a person, a city, a scenario the listener can picture: "Take a student working part-time in Vancouver..."
  4. Reasons (≈30 s): Develop your two reasons with because-clauses — this is the analytical heart and should take the largest block of time.
  5. Wider view (≈20 s): Zoom out to why it matters broadly — society, the future, fairness — and restate your position in one final confident line.
Timing reality: 90 seconds on ONE position feels long. Build a mental ladder: personal example → reason → second reason → wider significance → conclusion. Never restart your opinion to kill time.
3 Useful language

Useful Language

Stating your opinion
  • In my opinion, ... / I'm convinced that...
  • From my perspective, ...
  • There's no question in my mind that...
  • I take the view that...
Examples
  • A good example of this is...
  • Take... as an example — ...
  • I've seen this firsthand: at my workplace, ...
  • This happened to me when...
Reasons
  • The main reason for this is that...
  • Another important factor is...
  • What makes this so important is...
  • This is because... / The reason behind that is...
Wider view + conclusion
  • Looking at the bigger picture, ...
  • On a societal level, ...
  • This issue will only grow more important as...
  • For all these reasons, I firmly stand by my position that...
Linking words

Furthermore, However, Therefore, Not only... but also — one linker per idea, never stacked.

Stress these words

your position word (STRONGLY, UNEQUIVOCALLY) and each reason's key noun — that's what shows conviction.

4 Common traps

Common Traps

⚠ Trap 1 — Restating instead of answering

How it feels: "This is an interesting question about screen time that many parents wonder about..." — 30 seconds of wind-up, no position.

Instead: Sentence one is the answer: "I firmly agree that..." The prompt never needs repeating — they wrote it.

⚠ Trap 2 — One reason stretched thin

How it feels: The same point rephrased four times across 90 seconds — repetitive, and it shows.

Instead: Two distinct reasons planned in prep, each with its own example. Ladder up: reason → example → reason → wider view.

⚠ Trap 3 — Sounding undecided

How it feels: "Some people think this, others think that, and both have valid points..." — nobody asked for a survey.

Instead: Acknowledge the other side in half a sentence AT MOST ("While some worry about..., I still believe...") and return to your position.

⚠ Trap 4 — Finishing at 60 seconds

How it feels: Your conclusion lands at 60... then 30 seconds of "so yeah, that's why" padding.

Instead: Budget for 90: opinion 10s, two reason blocks 25–30s each, wider view 15s, conclusion 10s. Write the ladder in prep.

5 Practice

Original Practice Prompt

Original SelfPIP prompt — not an official CELPIP question

Statement: "Large cities should ban private cars from their downtown cores and give the space to pedestrians, cyclists and public transit."

Task: Do you agree or disagree? Give your opinion and support it with reasons and examples. (30 s preparation → 90 s response)

How to use this prompt: During prep, write two words only — your two reasons. During the recording, watch for Trap 1 and Trap 3 above: sentence one must be your position, and each reason needs its own example.
6 Model response

Annotated Model Response

"I strongly agree that large cities should close their downtown cores to private cars — I think it's one of the best moves a city can make.

Take a concrete example: when a city like Vancouver closes a major street to cars for a summer festival, thousands of people show up, shops along the street report higher sales, and the air literally smells different by the afternoon. That's not a theory — it happens every time.

There are two real reasons behind my position. The first is public health: car traffic is a leading source of urban air pollution, and every year of reduced emissions means fewer children with asthma and fewer elderly residents with breathing problems. The second is fairness of space — right now, one person in a two-tonne vehicle consumes far more road space than fifty people on bicycles or a bus full of commuters. Giving that space back to people simply distributes it more sensibly.

Looking at the bigger picture, this isn't anti-car — it's pro-choice: people would still drive where driving genuinely makes sense, while getting a downtown that's quieter, safer and actually pleasant to walk through. For all those reasons, I firmly believe the ban is the right direction, and cities that have tried it are not looking back."

Why this works

Opinion"I strongly agree..." — sentence one answers the question. Zero restating of the prompt.
ExamplesImmediately grounds the position in a picture the listener can see — festivals, higher sales, changed air.
ReasonsTwo DISTINCT reasons with because-logic: health (asthma) and fairness (space per person). No reason is recycled.
Wider viewZooms out ("pro-choice, not anti-car"), pre-empts the counterargument, and restates the position — a confident 90-second landing.
7 Self-check

Self-Check Before You Record

  • Did I avoid restating the prompt?
  • Did I give TWO distinct reasons, each developed?

  • Did I zoom out to the wider view before closing?

0 unchecked

Mindset

The test doesn't care WHICH side you take — it cares that you commit and support it. Say it like you'd say it to a friend at a café: direct, reasoned, and with one example they'd nod at.
