Reading Part 1

Reading Correspondence

Read personal or professional correspondence and understand the purpose, relationships, important details, tone, and implied meaning.

Section 1 — What Is This Part? Official format: 11 questions SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: Purpose → People → Details → Paraphrase → Context

What is this part?

In Part 1 you read correspondence — a personal message or a professional email — and answer questions about the communication itself: why it was written, who it is for, what it says, and what it implies between the lines.

The task tests careful reading of everyday writing. A single message carries facts, a request and a tone at the same time, so different questions can be answered by the same few lines in different ways.

In everyday terms: someone sends you a message, and you answer questions about what they said, what they want, and how they said it.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: Part 1 is the first of the four Reading tasks and officially contains 11 questions. The strategies, traps and examples on this page are SelfPIP study guidance — they are not official CELPIP rules.

What Should I Look For?

SelfPIP focus points

Read the message once for the situation, then look for these seven targets. Together they cover what correspondence questions usually ask about.

A

Who is writing?

The sender and their role.

A manager, a colleague, a friend or a company writes with a different purpose.

B

Who is receiving it?

One person, a team, or everyone?

The audience shapes how direct the message is.

C

Why was it written?

To inform, request, remind, apologize, or invite?

The purpose is usually visible in the first two sentences.

D

What happened?

The events behind the message.

Background sentences often hold the factual questions.

E

What does the writer want?

The request, and any deadline attached to it.

Requests are frequently paraphrased in the answer options.

F

Important details

Dates, times, places, names and numbers.

Precision questions live in these lines.

G

What does the tone suggest?

Formal, friendly, urgent, apologetic?

Tone questions ask how something is said, not just what.

3

Strategy

SelfPIP guidance

Work through the message in five steps. The first two are quick; the last two are where most answers are decided.

1

Identify the relationship

Who is communicating with whom?

A note from a supervisor reads differently from a note between friends — and the options often test exactly this.

2

Find the purpose

Why was the message written?

Say it to yourself in a few words: "to warn", "to arrange", "to apologize".

3

Track the important information

Names, dates, places, numbers — not every sentence.

Mark the details the way you would in a listening task: quickly and lightly.

4

Watch for paraphrases

The answer may express the same idea using different words.

"Bring your own mug" can appear as "use personal cups" — the meaning matches.

5

Check the complete context

Do not choose an answer based on one matching word.

Read the sentence before and after the match; both can change it.

The five moves to remember

Identify the relationship
↓
Find the purpose
↓
Track the important details
↓
Match paraphrases, not words
↓
Confirm with the full context
"The answer must be supported by the message itself — not by what you would write in its place."

Common Traps

SelfPIP guidance

⚠ Trap 1 — Matching identical words

How it feels"The option uses the same word as the email, so it must be right."

InsteadCompare the whole sentence: identical words can hide a different meaning.

⚠ Trap 2 — Confusing the request with the reason

How it feels"They asked for extra time because the printer broke."

InsteadSeparate what the writer wants from why they want it — questions often ask for only one.

⚠ Trap 3 — Missing the tone

How it feels"It lists rules, so the writer must be annoyed."

InsteadRead the openings and closings ("Thanks for your patience…") — they usually set the real tone.

⚠ Trap 4 — Choosing what is possible, not what is written

How it feels"That could be true, so I will pick it."

InsteadAsk where the option is supported. If nothing in the message says it, eliminate it.

⚠ Trap 5 — Ignoring who is writing to whom

How it feels"The message says we — so it must include the reader."

InsteadFix the sender and receiver first; pronouns and requests only make sense with them.

Remember: these traps are SelfPIP study guidance, not official rules. Use them to review your own habits after practice.

Original Mini Example

Original practice

This is an original SelfPIP example, not a CELPIP passage. Read the email once for the situation, then answer the questions — nothing is revealed until you choose an option.

From:
Priya Raman, Facilities Coordinator
To:
All Riverside Office staff — third floor
Subject:
Kitchen renovation: temporary arrangements (November 3–21)

Hello everyone,

The third-floor kitchen will be renovated from Monday, November 3 to Friday, November 21. During the renovation, the microwave and the shared fridge will move to the second-floor break room, which is open to our floor at any time.

Two things to plan around. First, the hot-water dispenser will be disconnected until November 10, so vending machines will be the only source of hot drinks during the first week. Second, please bring your own mug for the whole construction period — the dishwasher will be off, and washing cups in the washroom sinks is not allowed.

Everything should return to normal the week of November 24, followed by a deep clean on December 1. If the dates cause a problem for you, let me know before Friday and I will look at options.

Thanks for your patience while the work is done.

Priya

Question 1 — What is the main purpose of this email?

Question 2 — Until when are staff asked to bring their own mug?

Question 3 — What will be available in the second-floor break room during the renovation?

Question 4 — What best describes the tone of the message?

What to notice: several questions needed the same paragraph, and the tempting options repeated real words from the email (December 1, vending machines, rules). In Part 1, attaching the right detail to the right request is what separates the supported answer from the word-match.
