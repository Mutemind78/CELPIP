CELPIP Speaking · Task 3

Describing a Scene

Describe an image clearly and systematically so that a listener can understand what is happening and where things are located.

30 s preparation 60 s response SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: Start with the big picture, then move step by step — position every detail you name, so the listener can "see" the image.
1 What this task is

What Is This Task?

In Task 3 a photograph appears on the screen and you describe it out loud. The prompt asks you to describe what you see — the people, objects, setting, and activity in the image.

Your listener cannot see the picture, so your description has to carry it. Move systematically — overview first, then foreground to background, left to right — and attach a location to everything you mention. The same image is reused in Task 4, so a clear mental map here pays off twice.

Preparation

30 seconds — scan the image and choose your travel route through it.

Response

60 seconds — present tense, moving in one consistent order.

Same image

returns in Task 4 — locations you note now become clues later.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: The task description and timings above are official CELPIP format information. The WHAT + WHERE framework, language banks, traps, practice prompt, and model response on this page are SelfPIP study guidance — not official CELPIP material, and not a scoring rule.
2 How to answer

How to Answer This Task

The hub's WHAT + WHERE framework keeps your description systematic instead of random:

Who / What → Where → Action → Perspective
  1. Preparation (30 s): Choose your route before you speak: overview → left to right → foreground to background (or the reverse). Stick to it.
  2. Overview (≈5 s): One sentence that frames everything: "This photo shows a busy park on a sunny afternoon."
  3. Who / What (≈15 s): Name the main people and objects — three to five is plenty. Say what each one IS, not every detail about it.
  4. Where (≈20 s): Attach a position to every item: in the foreground, to the left of, behind, in the top right corner. Position words are the backbone of this task.
  5. Action (≈15 s): What are they doing right now? Present continuous: "is kicking a ball," "are sitting at a table."
  6. Perspective (≈5 s): A light closing line that gives the overall impression: "Overall, it looks like a relaxed, cheerful scene."
Timing reality: 60 seconds disappears fast. Pick a fixed route in preparation — deciding WHERE to look while speaking is what makes people stall.
3 Useful language

Useful Language

Overview opener
  • The image shows / depicts...
  • This is a picture of... taken probably at...
  • At first glance, it looks like...
Position words (Where)
  • in the foreground / in the background
  • to the left / to the right of...
  • behind, next to, in front of, above, below
  • in the top-right corner / along the far wall
  • stretching across the middle of the scene
Actions (What is happening)
  • is/are + -ing: "is carrying a backpack"
  • appears to be / seems to be + -ing
  • is about to / looks ready to (imminent action)
  • has just (action that just finished)
Guiding the listener + closing
  • On the left side of the image we can see...
  • If we move to the right, there's...
  • Overall, the scene gives the impression of...
  • All in all, it looks like a typical...
Stress these words

the nouns and the position words — "TABLE" and "BEHIND the sofa" carry the picture.

Avoid listing

never say two items without a position or an action between them.

4 Common traps

Common Traps

⚠ Trap 1 — No overview sentence

How it feels: You dive straight into "There's a man with a red hat..." — the listener has no frame to hang details on.

Instead: Spend the first 5 seconds framing it: "This photo shows a busy street market." Details land better after context.

⚠ Trap 2 — Listing without positions

How it feels: "There's a dog, a bench, a tree, and two kids." — items floating in space; the listener can't assemble the picture.

Instead: Every item gets a location: "In the foreground, a dog is sleeping NEXT TO a bench." Position is the backbone.

⚠ Trap 3 — Random eye movement

How it feels: Left side → far background → your hand → right foreground — your listener gets lost and stops following.

Instead: Pick one route during the 30-second prep (left→right, front→back) and travel it once, in order.

⚠ Trap 4 — Stuck on one detail

How it feels: 30 seconds describing one person's outfit while half the image goes unmentioned.

Instead: Three to five elements, evenly spread. When in doubt, widen: add the setting and overall atmosphere.

5 Practice

Original Practice Prompt

Original SelfPIP prompt — not an official CELPIP question

Task: Describe this image in detail. (30 s preparation → 60 s response)

How to use this prompt: Choose your travel route BEFORE you start recording. Every item you name must carry a position word — if you can delete a position word, that item isn't placed yet.
6 Model response

Annotated Model Response

"This photo shows a lively outdoor street market on what looks like a bright afternoon.

In the foreground, a group of shoppers walks along the grey pavement — one man in a blue jacket on the left, and in the centre a woman in a green coat who appears to be talking to a small child beside her. Slightly to the right of them, an older woman in a purple jacket is walking towards the right edge of the frame.

In the middle ground on the left, there's a market stall with a red-striped awning, and under it you can see boxes of oranges and green apples. On the far right of the scene there's a second stall with a blue awning displaying what looks like folded fabrics or clothes.

Between the two stalls, a busker with a guitar is playing for the passers-by. Overall, it gives the impression of a relaxed, cheerful weekend market."

Why this works

OverviewFirst sentence frames place + time + mood — the listener now has a stage to put details on.
Who / WhereEvery person arrives WITH a position: "in the foreground," "on the left," "to the right." Nothing floats.
RouteTravels one consistent path — foreground → middle ground left → right → between — instead of jumping around.
ActionPresent continuous throughout: "is walking," "appears to be talking," "is playing" — actions live in the now.
PerspectiveCloses with the overall impression — a proper ending rather than a description that just stops.

Mindset

You are the listener's eyes. If you say it with position and order, they SEE it — and a describable picture is a high-scoring picture. Calm beats clever.
