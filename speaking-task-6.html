CELPIP Speaking · Task 6

Dealing with a Difficult Situation

Explain how you would resolve a conflict or handle tough news calmly, respectfully, and with a workable solution.

60 s preparation 60 s response SelfPIP study guide
Golden rule: Stay calm and respectful — acknowledge the other side, clarify the real issue, propose a fair solution, and close politely.
1 What this task is

What Is This Task?

In Task 6 the prompt describes a delicate situation — a disagreement, a complaint you need to raise, bad news to deliver, or a problem someone brings to you. You explain how you would deal with it, step by step, while keeping the relationship intact.

What matters is HOW as much as what. The prompt wants a calm, adult approach: understanding the other person's side, naming the actual problem without blame, suggesting something fair, and ending on good terms. One minute means one clear plan, not a list of everything that could go wrong.

Preparation

60 seconds — the longest prep: write down all five TACT beats.

Response

60 seconds — walk through your plan in the order you would act.

Tone check

calm and respectful — the manner is part of the answer.

Official format vs SelfPIP advice: The task description and timings above are official CELPIP format information. The TACT framework, language banks, traps, practice prompt, and model response on this page are SelfPIP study guidance — not official CELPIP material, and not a scoring rule.
2 How to answer

How to Answer This Task

The hub's TACT framework is a five-step script for staying calm and effective:

Tone → Acknowledge → Clarify → Together → Thank
  1. Preparation (60 s): Use the full minute — write the five TACT beats as one word each, plus the specific facts of the situation (who, what, when).
  2. Tone (opening line): Set a calm register before any content: "I'd like to talk this through calmly, because I think there's an easy fix."
  3. Acknowledge (≈10 s): Show the other side you understand THEIR point first — it lowers defensiveness and marks you as reasonable: "I completely understand that you were in a hurry and..."
  4. Clarify (≈15 s): Name the real issue factually, without blame — this is where most people fail by starting with accusation.
  5. Together (≈25 s): The core — a specific, fair proposal: "What if we...? That way both of us..." Give the reasoning behind it.
  6. Thank (≈10 s): Close the relationship loop: "Thanks for hearing me out — I really appreciate it."
Timing reality: 60 seconds with a five-part framework means ~12 seconds per beat. Prepare the solution clearly in your 60-second prep — improvising it live is what causes rambling.
3 Useful language

Useful Language

Setting a calm tone
  • I'd like to start by saying how much I appreciate...
  • I know this is a sensitive topic, but I think it's worth discussing.
  • I'm bringing this up because I value our relationship, not to complain.
Acknowledging the other side
  • I completely understand where you're coming from.
  • I can see that you had your reasons for...
  • I realise this may be inconvenient for you, too.
Clarifying the real issue (no blame)
  • The way I see it, the issue is simply...
  • Just to make sure we're on the same page — the concern is...
  • My only worry is that...
Proposing a solution + closing
  • What if we...? That way, both sides...
  • Would you be open to...?
  • I'm sure we can work something out if...
  • Thanks so much for taking the time to talk this through.
Softening language

would, could, might, "I was wondering if..." — polite modals are how native speakers raise friction topics.

Never say

"You always...", "This is your fault" — absolute blame language turns a solution into a fight.

4 Common traps

Common Traps

⚠ Trap 1 — Opening with blame

How it feels: "You messed up my order again and I'm tired of it." — defensiveness switch flipped in the first second.

Instead: Tone first, issue second: "Hey, I wanted to mention something — I know you're busy, so..."

⚠ Trap 2 — Acknowledgement with no solution

How it feels: Beautiful empathy for 45 seconds — "I totally understand, I really appreciate..." — then time's up, problem unsolved.

Instead: Acknowledge in ONE sentence and spend the middle of the response on a concrete, fair proposal.

⚠ Trap 3 — Vague fixes

How it feels: "We should work together and communicate better" — sounds mature, says nothing actionable.

Instead: Name the mechanism: "What if we swap Tuesday and Thursday shifts? That way you still get your weekend free."

⚠ Trap 4 — Ignoring the "Thank" beat

How it feels: You deliver the solution and stop dead — the exchange ends on transaction, not relationship.

Instead: One closing line — "Thanks so much for hearing me out" — completes the TACT loop and sounds naturally polite.

5 Practice

Original Practice Prompt

Original SelfPIP prompt — not an official CELPIP question

Situation: Your roommate has started leaving his dirty dishes in the sink for days. You've both agreed to share cleaning duties, and you're frustrated, but the living situation is otherwise great and you don't want tension at home.

Task: Explain how you would deal with this situation. Describe what you would say and how you would handle the conversation. (60 s preparation → 60 s response)

How to use this prompt: Write the five TACT words during prep and check them off as you speak. If your "Together" step has no specific mechanism (who does what, when), rewrite it before you record again.
6 Model response

Annotated Model Response

"I'd open the conversation in a really low-key way — something like, 'Hey, do you have two minutes? Just a small house thing.' Keeping the tone casual matters, because this isn't a big deal and I don't want it to feel like a confrontation.

Then I'd acknowledge his side first: 'I know you've been slammed at work lately, and I totally get that some evenings the last thing you want to do is dishes.' That's true, and saying it out loud takes the edge off.

Next I'd clarify the actual issue without blame: 'The thing is, when dishes sit for a couple of days, the sink backs up and honestly it starts to smell — and it's not really fair on either of us.'

For the solution, I'd propose something concrete: 'What if we just agree that anything left in the sink after bedtime gets washed that night — or I'll handle it if you've had a brutal day, and we just keep an eye on each other?' That way the rule is simple, there's no score-keeping, and we're on the same team.

And I'd close by thanking him: 'Thanks for hearing me out — I really don't want this to be a thing between us.' I think he'd take it well, because the way you bring something up decides half the outcome."

Why this works

ToneOpens with the actual words she'd say, kept casual — the manner models the message.
Acknowledge"I know you've been slammed..." — his reality validated BEFORE any complaint. Defensiveness lowered.
ClarifyNames the real problem (smell, fairness) with zero blame words — "it starts to smell," not "you let it stink."
TogetherA specific mechanism with a built-in escape valve ("if you've had a brutal day") — fair, memorable, collaborative.
ThankCloses the relationship loop, then reflects on WHY the tone worked — a mature final thought.
7 Self-check

Self-Check Before You Record

Task 6 — Dealing with a Difficult Situation

  • Did I open with a calm, low-key tone?
  • Did I acknowledge the other person's side first?
  • Did I name the real issue without blame words?
  • Is my solution specific — who does what, when?
  • Did I close with a thank-you / relationship line?
  • Did I hit all five TACT beats inside 60 seconds?

Mindset

The calmest voice in the room wins this task. TACT isn't a script to rush through — it's proof you can handle friction like an adult, which is exactly what the test wants to hear.
