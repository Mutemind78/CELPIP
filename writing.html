Master professional emails and survey responses by learning how to organize ideas, develop examples, control grammar, use precise vocabulary, and match the required tone.

Start here

The CELPIP Writing component contains two tasks. Each task has a different purpose, so the learner should understand the task before writing. The same approach does not work equally well for an email and for a survey response.

  1. Understand the task
  2. Plan your response
  3. Organize your ideas
  4. Develop with reasons and examples
  5. Edit before submitting

The Core Writing Strategy

Five moves do most of the work in every Writing task. Learn what each one is for, and when to switch between them.

Understand

Read the prompt carefully before writing anything, and identify what you must accomplish.

  • who you are writing to
  • why you are writing
  • what the situation is
  • what you must include

Plan

Decide your purpose, your main points, and the order of your response before you write.

  • main purpose
  • main ideas
  • supporting details
  • examples

Organize

Use a logical structure so the reader can follow your ideas without effort.

  • clear paragraphs
  • logical order
  • transitions that fit
  • one idea per paragraph

Develop and check

Explain your ideas with reasons and examples, then review for task fulfillment, grammar, and tone.

  • develop, don't just list
  • use relevant examples
  • check task requirements
  • proofread before submitting
Clear and complete beats complicated and confusing. Never sacrifice clarity just to sound advanced.

How to choose what to write

  1. Read the prompt and identify what must be accomplished.
  2. Decide your purpose, position, or main request.
  3. Choose reasons or details that actually support it.
  4. Organize them into a clear sequence.
  5. Write, then check that every required point is covered.
Answer every required point. A response that is mostly correct but missing one required detail is often weaker than a complete, simpler response.

Time management

The official Writing component runs approximately 53 minutes, and the two tasks are not equally long. Treat the steps below as a practised routine rather than a fixed timing rule—there is no official per-task time limit.

  1. Understand the task before writing.
  2. Plan your main points quickly.
  3. Write one task at a time, staying on topic.
  4. Leave time to review both responses.
  5. Check for missing requirements and errors.
  6. Move forward once the response is complete.
Practice mode: when practicing, gradually train yourself to write complete, accurate responses while reducing the time spent planning and re-reading.

What if I don't know the right word?

Unfamiliar words are normal. Work outwards from the word instead of stopping:

  1. Read the surrounding sentence.
  2. Look for examples or explanations.
  3. Look at contrast words.
  4. Infer the approximate meaning from context.
  5. Ask whether the exact word is actually necessary to answer the task.
  6. Do not stop for every unfamiliar word.
You do not need to know every word to write well. A response built from clear, accurate, simpler language is stronger than one that forces complicated vocabulary where it does not fit.

Writing golden rules

1 Understand the task before writing.
2 Answer every required point.
3 Develop ideas instead of simply listing them.
4 Use examples that actually support your point.
5 Organize paragraphs logically.
6 Use precise, natural vocabulary.
7 Use grammar to make ideas clear.
8 Match your tone to the audience and situation.
9 Leave time to edit.
10 Never sacrifice clarity just to sound advanced.

Final writing checklist

Before you write

  • Understood who I am writing to.
  • Understood why I am writing.
  • Understood the situation.
  • Identified every required point.

While writing

  • Kept each paragraph focused.
  • Developed my reasons with examples.
  • Used vocabulary that fits naturally.
  • Matched my tone to the reader.

Before submitting

  • Answered every required point.
  • Checked grammar and punctuation.
  • Checked spelling.
  • Was within the required range.

If you cannot support a point or an idea, revise it before submitting.
